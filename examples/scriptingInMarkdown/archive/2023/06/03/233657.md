![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2020/01/16/rodneyDangerfield.png)I borrowed a lot of [prior art](http://essaysfromexodus.scripting.com/priorArtDesignMethod) for [FeedLand](https://docs.feedland.com/gettingstarted.md) from social media, specifically, the idea that subscription lists are public. So you can walk a network of followship, and get ideas of who to subscribe to that way. Click a checkbox and you're connected.

That makes it easy to [peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peering) with social networks, if they support OPML subscription lists and [RSS](https://cyber.harvard.edu/rss/rss.html) feeds of posts. And -- as of [May 24](http://scripting.com/2023/05/24.html#a135024), we now have that implemented for [Bluesky](https://www.google.com/search?q=site%3Ascripting.com+%22blue+sky%22&rlz=1C5CHFA_enUS743US747&oq=site%3Ascripting.com+%22blue+sky%22), through their API.

Everything's ready to go!

I've spent the last few days teaching FeedLand how to do with Bluesky what it does with old school RSS feeds. Today I got it working. Real sense of accomplishment. Not deployed yet, but I'm pretty sure it will be soon. The [pieces](https://www.smallpieces.com/) fit together amazingly well.

If you're a regular FeedLand user who also uses Bluesky, please respond to [this post](https://staging.bsky.app/profile/scripting.com/post/3jxbljxkizc2f). I'd like to loop you in on the new stuff when it's ready for testing. You don't actually need a Bluesky account to get the benefit, that's the cute/cool thing about it. We're bringing Bluesky to the open web. 😀

![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2022/07/26/motherOfDragons.png)I remember when I was a Comp Sci student at [UW](https://www.cs.wisc.edu/) taking the Compiler Design class, [CS701](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~fischer/cs701.f05/lectures/Lecture01.pdf). It was the class that weeded out those who would get graduate degrees from those who would not. The class project was to write a compiler for an [Algol-like language](https://web.archive.org/web/20111114122103/http://www.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/~ohearn/Algol/intro.html) that the professor desgined. He gave us a set of test apps that we had to compile and run correctly. I didn't get it. I thought code generation was like a magic box. Somehow the machine had a copy of every program you could write, and when it saw you were asking for that one, it would just spit out the code. Knowing what I know now, this was a ludicrous idea, but it's the best your mind can do when you just don't get it. Then there was the moment when I did get it. I started writing a code generator for a [while loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/While_loop), and plugged it into the [compiler-compiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiler-compiler), and saw what it generated worked. From that point on, it was just writing code. Same thing with the web. I thought of big data centers, until I visited one of the most popular sites on the web, [IUMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Underground_Music_Archive), and found it was just an old Sun terminal and a [pizza box computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizza_box_form_factor), sitting on the floor in a random dusty one-room office in [Santa Cruz](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Santa+Cruz,+CA/@36.9758914,-122.04748,13z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x808e441b7c36d549:0x52ca104b2ad7f985!8m2!3d36.9741171!4d-122.0307963!16zL20vMGwwbWs). That's it? I couldn't believe it. So I'm sure there's a [gestalt](https://www.thefreedictionary.com/gestalt) like that for [ChatGPT](https://chat.openai.com/chat). Behind the magic is a bit of software, as magical (or not) as my compiler, or the pathetic Santa Cruz machine with the huge net presence. I'm sure there's something very mundane behind the [curtain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NZR64EF3OpA), but for now I love the magic of it and I don't believe the explanations that say it's stupid. It feels really smart to me.

I have a rule that I don't point to Facebook posts. Rules were meant to be [broken](https://www.facebook.com/dave.winer.12/posts/635426309998111?notif_id=1507478092757946&notif_t=feedback_reaction_generic). It's a story I posted three years ago, about the time I pointed to a [story](http://nakedjen.smallpict.com/2014/10/07/itsAWayToTellYourStoryToShareWhatYouSeeToProcessItDrawConclusionsAndMoveOn.html) written by NakedJen. Read all the way to the end, please.

I just got a lecture from Facebook on their community standards.

1.  First they logged me off everywhere, requiring me to re-enter my password on every system I use Facebook on.
    
2.  Then a [dialog](http://static.scripting.com/larryKing/images/2014/10/08/weRemovedSomething.gif) saying they removed something I posted. (Okay fine, I'll click the button.)
    
3.  That's not enough. We want you to [review](http://static.scripting.com/larryKing/images/2014/10/08/review.gif) the community standards. And then click another button to indicate that you heard us.
    
4.  What was the offense? It's so silly. A couple of nipples attached to a simple and lovely very Facebookish [post](http://nakedjen.smallpict.com/2014/10/07/itsAWayToTellYourStoryToShareWhatYouSeeToProcessItDrawConclusionsAndMoveOn.html) by NakedJen.
    

BTW, it looks like she removed the picture. It was a picture of NJ on the stairway at my old house in Berkeley, naked of course, but with her legs crossed, and arms spread out in the usual NJ way. They have to have their standards, I understand, and nipples are a no-no, this picture crossed the line, but..

Get this:

It was THEIR choice to grab the picture. Not mine. I just linked to her post. Maybe they should lecture their bot, not me.

Postscript, in 2017

I don't blame Facebook for this. They tried best they could to keep porn off their site. In my country nipples are considered pornographic. Their software hadn't learned yet to distinguish between photos scraped by their bot, vs photos posted by users.

The moral of the story. When you want Facebook to stop political ads aimed at Americans from Russia, that means we're all going to be subjected to this kind of interrogation, not just about nipples, but also about our political values. People who believe you can cut out Russians without interfering with normal speech are naive about how the technology works.

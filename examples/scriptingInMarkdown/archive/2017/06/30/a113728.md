![](http://static.scripting.com/larryKing/images/2013/06/06/michaelJordan.gif)A year ago today the new Knicks coach [said](http://www.nydailynews.com/sports/basketball/knicks/knicks-coach-ny-isn-rebuilding-city-win-now-town-article-1.2694767?utm_content=buffer90dbf&utm_medium=social&utm_source=twitter.com&utm_campaign=NYDNSports+Twitter) the Knicks are a _win-now_ team. So they shipped off three healthy players in a [trade](http://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/knicks-trade-for-derrick-rose-send-lopez-grant-to-bulls-grades-takeaways/) for [two players](http://nypost.com/2017/04/05/two-ex-knicks-in-derrick-rose-trade-finding-niche-in-chicago/) with too many miles on their legs. A replay of the disastrous win-now [strategy](http://ftw.usatoday.com/2017/05/nba-draft-lottery-2017-boston-celtics-why-lottery-pick-awful-trade-brooklyn-nets-kevin-garnett-paul-pierce-breakdown) of the Nets, which [now](https://www.sbnation.com/2017/6/26/15875468/d-angelo-russell-press-conference-nets-future-lottery-pick) have them in solid rebuilding mode. Win-now is a shitty strategy, esp for the Knicks. Never has a team needed more to see itself as a work in progress. The thing the win-now proponents don't understand is that fans _like_ teams with long-term strategies. They might bitch and moan about not winning or contending for a title, but a team with hope draws fan [interest](http://scripting.com/2015/02/25/thisThisIsWhyWeLikeBasketball.html). A foolish team that makes shitty short-term bets? Well there are always other sports to watch. :baseball:

![](http://static.scripting.com/larryKing/images/2014/05/25/goodHumor.gif)An interesting [comment](https://github.com/kylewm/woodwind/issues/7#issuecomment-311000922) from [Chris Aldrich](https://github.com/chrisaldrich) about subscribing to lists of feeds in a thread on the [Woodwind](https://github.com/kylewm/woodwind) app site on GitHub.

Here's the basic idea. There's a difference between importing OPML into a reader and subscribing to it. The latter is very powerful, for the user, but a lot of RSS reader devs may not want their users to have that much power. It's not a very hard feature to implement.

The idea has been [much-discussed](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=site%3Ascripting.com+%22reading+list%22&t=hz&ia=web) here. We call them reading lists. Michael Arrington even wrote a [TechCrunch piece](https://techcrunch.com/2005/10/16/my-thoughts-on-reading-lists/) about it in 2005.

Subscribable OPML is something all my readers have been able to do through an OPML feature called [inclusion](http://dev.opml.org/spec2.html#inclusion). I wrote a [howto](https://github.com/scripting/river5/blob/master/docs/DROPBOXSUBSCRIPTIONLISTS.md) for a [River5](https://github.com/scripting/river5) user re inclusion just last week.

[Share Your OPML](https://techcrunch.com/2006/05/07/share-your-opml/) was a service I operated for a while. It made it possible to manage your OPML separate from the reader you used. It was meant to encourage readers to support subscribable OPML. I'm looking for an excuse to bring it back, but first we need a base of shared feed lists.

A lot of good stuff can be done if feed readers are willing to delegate list management to other services. IMHO the only reason a reader developer _wouldn't_ do it is because they want to lock users in. If I let you edit your feed list elsewhere that means you could give the list to another vendor and have a choice which to use. It's really something users should demand, esp if you're paying for the service.

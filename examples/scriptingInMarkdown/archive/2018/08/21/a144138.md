![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2018/08/21/bloatwareBoss.png)I was talking with [Wes Felter](http://wmf.editthispage.com/) last night about a project I want to do. I want to get Frontier running more or less as-is on Linux. [Wes](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=%22wes+felter%22&t=hi&ia=twitter) was an early [UserLander](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=site%3Ascripting.com+wes+felter&t=hj&ia=web), he got us started with Frontier as an HTTP server. He understood the product at the time in ways I didn't. What came from that? [XML-RPC](http://xmlrpc.com/), the [website framework](http://scripting.com/frontier/netScripting/web.html), [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UserLand_Software#Manila), [Radio UserLand](http://radio.userland.com/) and lots of other stuff. We talked about how to get the project done, and he suggested doing video demos of the things that make Frontier unique. It's a really good idea. And kind of daunting. I'm thinking about how to get started. I think the first step is to think of a project I can do in steps, that touches on most aspects of the environment. Maybe a web site for [Bull Mancuso](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=%22bull+mancuso%22&t=hi&ia=web) or [Kim Parker](http://kim.smallpict.com/) of [Bloatware](http://bloatware.org/) (two not-so-famous fake people I use for demos and docs). It would be more interesting if it turned out to be something useful that made sense to deploy in Frontier. I wouldn't mind setting up a server on Amazon running Windows just to get the app up and running. And then getting that app running in Frontier on Linux would be the home run.

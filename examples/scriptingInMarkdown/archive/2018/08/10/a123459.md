I just looked for some of the old 2003-2004 sites, and they're already gone. Earlier this year we [lost the handle](http://scripting.com/2018/05/10/204408.html) on [Radio UserLand](http://radio-weblogs.com/) weblogs because the [new owner](http://www.weblogs.com/about.html) of weblogs.com was unwilling to maintain a [DNS entry](http://radio.weblogs.com/) pointing to them. That and Google's marking HTTP sites as [not secure](http://this.how/googleAndHttp/) have been huge blows to the web as an archival medium. It's a good time to pause and reflect on the question of what was the value of all the work, and why bother continuing if the people who should care, a major university, a large research company and one of the largest tech companies, don't care about maintaining the web. What hope is there for it being maintained in the future?

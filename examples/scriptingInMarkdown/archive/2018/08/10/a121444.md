![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2018/08/10/sadBeatles.png)I got an email in the middle of the night asking if I had seen an [announcement](https://blogs.harvard.edu/blog/2018/07/13/upcoming-changes/) from Berkman Center at Harvard that they will stop hosting blogs.harvard.edu. It's not clear what will happen to the archives. Let's have a discussion about this. That was the first academic blog hosting system anywhere. It was where we planned and reported on our Berkman Thursday meetups, and [BloggerCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BloggerCon). It's where the first podcasts were hosted. When we tried to figure out what makes a weblog a weblog, that's where the [result](http://blogs.harvard.edu/whatmakesaweblogaweblog.html) was posted. There's a lot of history there. I can understand turning off the creation of new posts, making the old blogs read-only, but as a university it seems to me that Harvard should have a strong interest in maintaining the archive, in case anyone in the future wants to study the role we played in starting up these (as it turns out) important human activities.

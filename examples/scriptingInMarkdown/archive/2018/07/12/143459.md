A Twitter account called [Black and Proud](https://twitter.com/BlkAndPr0ud/status/1016367865400832000) addresses white people:

_Please stop saying 'I don't see color.' Instead -- I see you and your struggle, because of your skin color. I will stand with you to end racism._

I agree. Race _is_ visible, and the statement that you don't see it is ludicrous. We see it. To say otherwise is to push it aside. I've written about this a few times before, once in an explainer about [Black Lives Matter](http://scripting.com/liveblog/users/davewiner/2016/01/17/0880.html), and what it means, from a white person's perspective.

I want to do more. I feel compelled, not just to make life safer and simpler for people of color, but also to put up a roadblock to racism, to let it know that it will encounter resistance. Some things are better kept under cover. Racism for sure is one of them.

![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2017/12/09/mlk.png)In the past I've [proposed](http://scripting.com/2016/12/30/theMlkButtonsArrived.html) that we all wear Martin Luther King buttons. I felt his [image](http://scripting.com/images/2017/12/09/mlk.png) was perfect because he's a black man, he preached non-violence, and was active, not passive, in his quest for equality and fairness. A white person wearing a [MLK button](http://scripting.com/images/2017/12/09/mlk.png) makes a statement of equivalence. Treat me as you would treat a black person. If a majority of whites wore these buttons it would make a promise to our fellow citizens, that if there's trouble, we're standing with you. _When_ there's trouble.

It would be like Article 5 in NATO. An attack on one of us is an attack on all of us. As a white person I can't become black. But I can make a statement that I stand with people of color.

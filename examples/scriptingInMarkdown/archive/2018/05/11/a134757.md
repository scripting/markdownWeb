![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2018/05/11/helloMyNameIsDave.png)Yesterday's [piece](http://scripting.com/2018/05/10/133513.html) about how the Internet is going in the wrong direction started as a set of notes for an interview I was doing with German public radio about the Angry Founders of the Internet [post](http://scripting.com/2018/05/06/183651.html). They got a kick out of it. It was meant to be funny, like [Angry Birds](https://www.angrybirds.com/). But what came out was one of the best summaries of [what the Internet is](http://scripting.com/2018/05/10/133513.html), what makes it important, and what needs to be preserved. In a way, a 2018 followup to JPB's [declaration](https://www.eff.org/cyberspace-independence). Main point: companies _must not_ take on the role of governments. When they do, disaster results. A thoughtful company would realize this. All the companies say they are thoughtful. Perhaps they should think a bit more and take a step back. Apple must stop trying to hijack web links. Google must live with the flaws in HTTP. Facebook must care about preserving what's good about the web (links, styles, titles, podcasts). And Medium must take steps to preserve their user's writing outside a paywall. All of them would do better, long-term, if they didn't consume what's great about the web, rather invested in making it even better at doing what it does so miraculously well.

[John Gilmore](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/John_Gilmore): "The Net interprets censorship as damage and routes around it."

![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2018/05/19/bigBrother.png)For the latest on Google's plans to turn the open web into Disneyland, read their May 17 [announcement](https://blog.chromium.org/2018/05/evolving-chromes-security-indicators.html). I've written a [backgrounder](http://this.how/googleAndHttp/) on the issue of Google and HTTP. Before jumping to any conclusions about what I think, please read it.

Anyway, I had an [idea](https://github.com/scripting/Scripting-News/issues/80) that there might be a route-around. Suppose there was a site that was a container for the web. It has an address bar at the top where you enter the address of a site. It then displays the contents of the site below. It accepts HTTP or HTTPS. But the container site is accessible via HTTPS.

Problem solved? It seems we've restored access to the sites Google doesn't like. Yes you have to access us in a compatibility box, and that's hopefully just a temporary fix until one of Google's competitors decides to try a more cooperative approach to whatever it is Google is trying to do (and I don't believe it has anything to do with our security, it's their ad revenue they're worried about).

PS: Here's a [place](https://github.com/scripting/Scripting-News/issues/80) to comment on the _technical_ merits of this idea. If you have a political statement please post it on your blog. Thanks.

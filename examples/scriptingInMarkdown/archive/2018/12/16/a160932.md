![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2018/12/16/rollerSkate.png)Lots of writing about the 50th anniversary of Engelbart's [MOAD](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=50th+anniversary+of+Engelbart%27s+Demo&t=h_&ia=web). I wasn't there, but I knew Engelbart, at first through Ted Nelson's [writing](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/722414.Computer_Lib_Dream_Machines), and then in face to face. The reporters misunderstand what he meant by [augmentation](http://dougengelbart.org/content/view/138/000/). It's the same thing we called idea processing with [ThinkTank](http://davewiner.userland.com/outlinersProgramming) and its [successors](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=%22living+videotext%22+outliners&t=h_&ia=web). Computers, using outlining software, are incredible surfaces for recording your thoughts, seeing them visually, and then manipulate them in ways you couldn't on physical surfaces like paper and whiteboards. Index cards spread out all over the floor kind of approximates it. On a computer screen ideas are on rollerskates and rails. They move fast and precisely and then move again. There are so many more places to move them. This frees the mind up to try out new ideas without losing the train of thought. All the stories about Engelbart miss this. Yes the mouse was incredible. I was not aware he invented the web (did he?). But the thing he was most interested in, the biggest idea he had, apparently has been forgotten, but not by me.

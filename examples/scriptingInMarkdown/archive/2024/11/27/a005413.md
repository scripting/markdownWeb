![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2022/08/20/brilliantFriend.png)Every [generation](https://x.com/davewiner/status/1861902063044804680) or two you hear about a few tech [wiener boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Boys%27_Choir) who think after getting founders stock at a Silicon Valley company and getting his picture in [Business Week](http://images.scripting.com/archiveScriptingCom/2007/05/09/bwquote.gif) or [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/27/marc-andreessen-joe-londsale-and-all-the-other-vcs-reportedly-in-the-running-for-new-trump-committees/) that he should run Africa or make up some country and for a minute tries doing it and no one pays much attention and a generation or two later it happens again, and the conclusion is that being a tech wiener boy doesn’t really prepare you to do anything but make a bunch of money once, be bored, and really nothing else. First time I witnessed this phenomenon was with a few product marketing [wünderbrats](https://www.wildcraftmeats.com/brat-type/wunderbrat/) at Apple in the early 80s, smart people (not kidding) but not nearly as smart as they thought they were. A big part was the serendipity of being in a [Hobee’s](https://hobees.com/locations/) or [Buck's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck%27s_of_Woodside) when Steve Jobs or a [Kleiner](https://www.kleinerperkins.com/our-history/) partner was having [breakfast](https://hobees.com/breakfast-menu/) after a [big IPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netscape#:~:text=Initial%20public%20offering%20\(IPO\),-The%20original%20green&text=On%20August%209%2C%201995%2C%20Netscape,to%20US%2428%20per%20share.).

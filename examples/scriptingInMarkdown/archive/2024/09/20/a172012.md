![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2024/02/01/runner.png)This is a test. For the next sixty seconds this station will conduct a test of a new server app that combines the functionality of several apps I used to run as separate servers. The goal is to be able to integrate the functionality of all the components. It's time to invest in blogging in a real way. The [2017 corner-turn](http://scripting.com/2017/05/05/iWantMyOldBlogBack.html) was about getting back to the foundation I had before trying to co-exist with Twitter, Facebook, Medium. All three are now no longer high value targets for interop. So while I wasted a lot of time trying to [peer](http://scripting.com/2024/09/08/152655.html) with them, I did finally get off the horse and I had a decent setup while developed [Drummer](http://drummer.land/) and [FeedLand](https://docs.feedland.com/gettingstarted.md) and in doing so learned how to do scalable server apps with [SQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQL). I've wasted so much time trying to be compatible with apps that didn't want us. I have a feeling that none of the current targets really want us either. So let's get back to the [Open Web](http://scripting.com/2015/06/24/keyConceptOfTheOpenWebWorkingTogether.html), which gave us much hope before. I think we can do a lot with feeds and [OPML](http://opml.org/). They're to open systems what [GPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_General_Public_License) is to open source. You don't get to be half-committed to being open, you have to be all-in or you can't play. (Same idea as [podcasting](https://www.google.com/search?q=site%3Ascripting.com+podcasting), which of course is just an instance of [RSS](https://cyber.harvard.edu/rss/rss.html).) You don't have to deal with a lot of confusion to find the interop. You just have to be willing to look in that direction. This started out as a test post and look what it has become.

I think you can save time by starting over sometimes, and rebuild the complexity, carefully testing at every step.

Going back and fixing a poorly tested corner-turn, esp one you did a while ago, is a time-consuming and frustrating process. You need to have a fully consistent model to evolve. When you try to reorganize a complicated bit of code, and you find yourself scratching your head about how this or that works, that's a sign that it _doesn't_ work.

But it's much more fun to add new features to a broken foundation than it is to either fix the foundation or rip it out and put in a new one.

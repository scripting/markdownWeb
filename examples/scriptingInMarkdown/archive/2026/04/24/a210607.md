![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2023/04/17/asGoodAsItGets.png)I want writing to be as [open as podcasting](https://textcasting.org/). The pattern is ridiculously easy to apply. If this were on a a high school math test, it would be too easy, everyone would get it right. How do you make text work like podcasting? 1. You look for a brain-dead obvious [choice](https://www.markdownguide.org/cheat-sheet/) for text. 2. And then attach it to a [format](https://cyber.harvard.edu/rss/rss.html) that's really good for transmitting packets of text. And then write software that works really well with the obvious choices. The user retains ownership and control of their writing, pays for the storage, and can give access to the apps they want to use. They can also, for a fee, point a domain name to one of the nodes in their storage. This would radically change the economics for independent developers. Now we don't have to resell storage. Products can be developed on our kitchen tables. There is an explosion of interest in developing software. Think it through -- how are they supposed to deploy their apps on the web? We need a BigCo that thinks like an entrepreneurial startup. How many times have I written [this screed](https://daytona.scripting.com/search?q=storage)? Geez I don't like to think about that.

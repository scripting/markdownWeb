![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2026/03/26/loveRss.png)[Yesterday](http://scripting.com/2026/05/11/144453.html) I learned about JSONL, and was of course intrigued. It's a really simple thing, even simpler than [RSS](https://cyber.harvard.edu/rss/rss.html), and does basically the same thing. And even better, it's the way the AI industry hooks streams together. So If we can get RSS to serve as a source of JSONL feeds, it's possible that the AI industry will find it useful. My goal is to get every standard of the web hooked up to AI, quickly, before the silos realize they're leaving out something important. Once they figure it out, they'll have no choice but to add real RSS support. So I put together a quick demo app that hooks into [FeedLand](https://github.com/scripting/feedlandSocket) and posts to a [JSONL feed](https://jsonldemo.feedland.org/items.jsonl) new items from one of a small set of feeds I chose basically at random. And here is [the JSONL feed](https://jsonldemo.feedland.org/items.jsonl). If you're a developer in AI-land could you try reading this into your JSONL-ingesting app, and let me know if I got it right. Here's a [place to comment](https://github.com/scripting/Scripting-News/issues/354). BTW, that URL is temporary just for this quick demo.

![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2022/03/13/beatles.png)I've been working my way through [The Beatles Anthology](https://www.amazon.com/Beatles-Anthology-John-Lennon/dp/B00008GKEG/ref=asc_df_B00008GKEG/?tag=hyprod-20&linkCode=df0&hvadid=312114908841&hvpos=&hvnetw=g&hvrand=14389232248250740513&hvpone=&hvptwo=&hvqmt=&hvdev=c&hvdvcmdl=&hvlocint=&hvlocphy=1023511&hvtargid=pla-499846321357&psc=1) videos, which you can only buy on CD or VHS tape. This material should be streamed, just like the [Get Back](https://www.disneyplus.com/series/the-beatles-get-back/) video was. The anthology incredibly long, includes interviews with all the Beatles. Goes through the whole history, in great detail, with concert performances that I had never seen, didn't even know existed. It's even more clear that the Beatles didn't "break up" as much as they stayed together for a long time. You get the sense of how exhausted they must've been of being a Beatle. This goes back to something I've learned myself in a career that included more than the normal amount of public presence. We all are just individual people, no matter how big the rep. It doesn't get much bigger than being a Beatle. But it doesn't really have [integrity](http://scripting.com/davenet/2001/01/08/whatIsIntegrity.html#3) to be a Beatle, because a human can't be that big. The disconnext has to be resolved somehow. We're all the same [size](http://scripting.com/2016/05/31/1296.html). Something has to give.

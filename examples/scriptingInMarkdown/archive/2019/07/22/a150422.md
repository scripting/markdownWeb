![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2019/07/22/rssShirt.png)I'm doing an interesting project, reviewing all the top-level pages on the [RSS 2.0](https://cyber.harvard.edu/rss/rss.html) spec, checking every link to see if it's broken. For those that are, I try to come up with a suitable replacement, usually the archive.org snapshot taken around the time the page was written. If it isn't broken I don't do anything. Here's an [OPML file](http://scripting.com/misc/rss2specbrokenlinks.opml) with all the links I've found. Most of the external links are gone, but there are some surprises, sites that have been well-preserved, for example [Jeremy Zawodny's](http://jeremy.zawodny.com/blog/archives/000967.html). I just came across a link to the [first podcast feed](http://xmlviewer.scripting.com/?url=http://static.userland.com/gems/backend/gratefulDead.xml), it's still there. I'm not sure how, but it is. This is a [restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration) project, started because there was breakage caused by the move to HTTPS on the server. That breakage has been fixed. I also changed the line-endings from carriage-return to linefeed, which now is the almost-universal standard. At the time, 2003, it was still kind of muddy. As a result you can now read the source of the pages in a current browser. The site is a relic. It's a good idea to fix the broken links every 15 years or so. :boom:

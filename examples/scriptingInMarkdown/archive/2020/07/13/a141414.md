![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2019/09/11/tide.png)We may be at the point in the unraveling of America when our favorite diners shut down. For me it was the [Odessa](https://gothamist.com/food/odessa-restaurant-late-night-east-village-oasis-close) on [Avenue A](https://www.google.com/maps/@40.7263911,-73.9833187,3a,75y,301.34h,92.34t/data=!3m7!1e1!3m5!1sjZYPzLkIfakUz6jzf8gGnw!2e0!6s%2F%2Fgeo3.ggpht.com%2Fcbk%3Fpanoid%3DjZYPzLkIfakUz6jzf8gGnw%26output%3Dthumbnail%26cb_client%3Dmaps_sv.tactile.gps%26thumb%3D2%26w%3D203%26h%3D100%26yaw%3D36.482285%26pitch%3D0%26thumbfov%3D100!7i16384!8i8192), across from [Tompkins Square Park](https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/tompkins-square-park), in the East Village. I used to meet my [mother](http://photos.scripting.com/2011/march/momorderingfromodessa) and grandfather there in the 80s. My uncle lived nearby in the hippie days, the late 60s and 70s. When I lived in NYC in the last decade, the teens, I'd [take](http://photos.scripting.com/2010/june/odessa) an out-of-town visiting friend maybe once a year. It wasn't the best of the Old World diners, but it was the last original one. [Veselka](http://scripting.com/stories/2010/08/05/lunchWithNakedjen.html) now is the goto place for that kind of food. The links to the old generation are disappearing, no surprise because the generation itself is gone. Not surprising that a place like Odessa can't survive just on takeout, when there are so many other great choices in the Village.

On Twitter, [Joe Pezillo says](https://twitter.com/ejoep/status/1265522849973927940) he's pretty sure Dave Winer (me) got the idea for [RSS](https://cyber.harvard.edu/rss/rss.html) from Newton. He's also pretty sure I won't admit it. He's wrong on the first count, and right on the second. :smile:

I wrote a tweet that was too long to fit in 280 chars that lists the steps that led to what we think of as RSS today. It wasn't an [invention](http://scripting.com/stories/2010/11/11/didYouInventRss.html) as most people think of invention, it was more of a synthesis, one idea leading to another and then another.

*   It started with seeing [Pointcast](http://scripting.com/davenet/1996/01/14/cnnonyourdesktop.html), being blown away by the idea.
    
*   Then a [guy](http://scripting.com/davenet/1997/11/25/Thanksgiving1997.html#4) from Microsoft wanted me to do something with XML, so I turned my [blog into a feed](http://my.userland.com/stories/storyReader$11).
    
*   Then Netscape reinvented what I did.
    
*   Then I said OK let's do it your way.
    
*   Then someone leaked the XML feeds for the NYT to me.
    
*   Then I repurposed them and published them so people could use them from Radio UserLand.
    
*   Then they shut me down (they were nice about it).
    
*   Then they [let me have them](http://scripting.com/davenet/2002/04/10/newYorkTimesAndUserland.html) for free.
    
*   Then we [turned](https://scripting.wordpress.com/2006/02/09/how-the-ny-times-came-to-support-rss/) them into [RSS](https://cyber.harvard.edu/rss/rss.html) feeds.
    
*   Then RSS took off like a bat out of hell! :boom:
    

And thus was born what we now think of as RSS.

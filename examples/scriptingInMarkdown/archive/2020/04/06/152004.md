![](http://static.scripting.com/larryKing/images/2013/08/07/ohRudy.gif)My maternal grandfather, [Rudy Kiesler](http://rudykiesler.com/), was a bundle of energy. Highly opinionated. Very loud. Didn't spend a lot of time thinking (or so it seemed to me as a child) he just burst into action.

He was the 13th of 13 children, born in the land between Poland and Germany, sent off to fend for himself as a very young boy.

Jewish, he was deported in the first rounds of the Nazi regime, leaving my grandmother and mother behind. Lucie, my grandmother, was Lutheran, blonde-haired, blue-eyed Aryan princess. Why they married is something I never understood. They didn't seem to have anything in common.

So the women were left in Germany or Czechoslavakia, I'm not clear on which, and [Rudy K](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=site%3Ascripting.com+rudy+kiesler&t=hy&ia=web) was in Brooklyn or Queens, setting up a garment business, which he ran until he had a stroke in the 1970s. With no heir wanting to inherit the business, he sold the company, United Pioneer, to [Salant & Salant](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/53/Salant-Corporation.html). But he was always a bundle of energy, until he drifted away in a nursing home and died in the early 90s.

He would not have liked this "stay home stay safe" thing. I can't imagine he would have done it. He was the kind of guy who couldn't stand to wait in a line at the [World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_New_York_World%27s_Fair), so he'd cut in front of the person at the front of the line, and when they complained, he'd ignore them (this actually happened).

Here's a [picture](http://scripting.com/images/2001/09/22/kieslers.gif) of his [family](http://scripting.com/2001/09/22.html#l60a1aad9dbba17d33466766294610e29) in Rockaway probably in 1961 or so.

He was a legend. Still looms large in my mind.

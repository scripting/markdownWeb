![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2022/12/19/ucsdPascalManual.png)October [blog posts](https://github.com/scripting/Scripting-News/blob/master/blog/opml/2025/10.opml) in [OPML](http://opml.org/). BTW, well-kept secret, I have a [Node app](https://github.com/scripting/oldSchoolBlog) that takes one of these outlines and publishes it to the web, in the format you see on [Scripting News](http://scripting.com/). If you can get your text to flow through OPML this way, you can have a replica of this site, with most of its features. And you can totally customize it in CSS. It's open source and very stable. It's called [Old School](https://github.com/scripting/oldSchoolBlog) and I wrote it in 2017 because I wanted my old blog back, I was fed up with trying to fit into the very small world defined by Twitter, Google Reader, Facebook and Medium. I decided then the cost was too high, and I'd be much happier if I lived within the limits of [RSS](https://cyber.harvard.edu/rss/rss.html), which is far better than the text formats defined by these other projects. Now I feel hopeful about this -- because [Brent](https://daytona.scripting.com/search?q=%22brent%20simmons%22) is supporting [source:markdown](https://daytona.scripting.com/search?q=source%3Amarkdown) in [NetNewsWire](https://netnewswire.com/). This is a big thing, because it will have the power to attract authors, developers of blogging systems, and other feed reading products.

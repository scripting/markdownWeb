One of the big flaws of in serial podcasts about TV shows is how the actors talk about how wonderful it is to work with all the specific people in the show, the other actors, directors, costume designers, and on and on. So if there are 100 people working on the show, this adds up to 10,000 testimonials, and they're sooooo boring because they're all the same, and I don't know any of these people, not even the actors. I know the characters in the show. And esp with a show like Severance, where each scene is filled with puzzles, and details you can't see on the first watching or even the fifth, there's so much you want them to talk about, and they never seem to get around to it.

The podcast for last week's Severance was the exception. What follows is a spoiler, so if you haven't seen it, you have been warned. For me, the most amazing scene was with the outies, Helena and Mark in what turned out to be Eng's restaurant in Kingston. It was so sexy, flirtatious, a tour de force of everything, and I don't often say that. And the funny thing is, the two bodies have had sex, twice, and only one of them knew about one of the events, and neither of them knew they had done it just a few hours ago! So the sexual attraction between them was real, and consummated. The best part I guess when they just replayed the dialog. So beautifully done, you don't need the video to be able to fully visualize it.

That's what I want -- go deeper into what happened between the characters I know and want to know more about. As soon as you start giggle, know that at least this member of your audience is getting ready to turn you off. (And btw the mutual adoration would be more interesting if they ever say they didn't like someone, but they always just love them, they're the best, they learned so much, I'm not worthy and on and on and on and on.)

This same thing happened a few days ago on the Wirecutter podcast where the people talking were reporters at the NYT who I had never heard of. Someone who reviews mattresses. I was interested in buying a mattress so I thought this would be a good podcast to listen to. But after 15 minutes they still hadn't talked about anything that helped me choose a mattress, which is something I don't know how to do, and I imagine most people don't either, yet it is an incredibly consequential decision. They may have eventually gotten to the point, but I wouldn't know.

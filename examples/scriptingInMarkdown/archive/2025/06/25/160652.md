I tried an experiment, [go back](https://imgs.scripting.com/2025/06/25/asfarasyoucango.png) as far as I can in archive.org on scripting.com and see where it gets me. The [first try](https://web.archive.org/web/19961114163115/http://scripting.com/) got me to a classified ads site I was experimenting with in mid-November 1996. A few weeks later, on [December 3,](https://web.archive.org/web/19961203224609/http://scripting.com/) there was a colorful directory that took you to all the sub-sites on the server, DaveNet, [Frontier](https://web.archive.org/web/19961203224624/http://www.scripting.com/frontier/), Classified ads, our Midas Website (Macintosh Internet Developers Association), DocServer, a BBS, and Guestbook.

I went digging around in the [Frontier](https://web.archive.org/web/19961203224624/http://www.scripting.com/frontier/) part. Lots of stuff there. By then we had been working on Frontier for eight years. It had been reborn once, from a Mac-focused single-user scripting environment to a networked one, all because the web had exploded and Apple didn't want us making system software for their platform. :smile:

BTW, some of this stuff is [still here](http://scripting.com/frontier/olddefault.html).

There are lots of paths to try out.

This was where my [blog home page](https://web.archive.org/web/19961203224710/http://www.scripting.com/frontier/news.html) was then.

Then to DaveNet, and in the left margin [Nerd's guide to this website](https://web.archive.org/web/19961203224727/http://www.scripting.com/davenet/aboutthiswebsite.html).

I love the screen shots that show what a good match the Frontier object database was to the way a website is organized.

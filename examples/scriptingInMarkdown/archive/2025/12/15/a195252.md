![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2025/12/15/reiner.png)For some reason, I'm hit especially hard by the death of [Rob Reiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Reiner). And it's coming at a time when I understand a lot more about how movie directors work, having watched the fantastic [Mr Scorsese](https://www.metacritic.com/tv/mr-scorsese/) 5-part [documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Scorsese) [series](https://www.metacritic.com/tv/mr-scorsese/) on Apple TV. The movie director can be as involved in the story as much as the writers or actors. There was a story about Reiner, I heard today in eulogy: he was dating his future wife at the same time he was directing the fantastic [When Harry Met Sally](https://www.metacritic.com/movie/when-harry-met-sally/). He changed the ending because he was in love, and thus created the most heart-pulling [end to a story](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q9JSVGS4dHU), when the two friends realize they should be together, and Billy Crystal's character gives the great closing speech that contains this line, that pretty well sums up the urgency of love: "When you realize you want to spend the rest of your life with somebody, you want the rest of your life to start as soon as possible." The Scorsese doc opens up the art of making movies for me in the same way the [Peter Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Jackson) [documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles:_Get_Back) on the [Beatles](http://scripting.com/2022/01/25/144824.html?title=imABeatleshead) showed us how super creative music creators do their work. And the timing is great, because it says so much about Reiner's accomplishments and gifts.

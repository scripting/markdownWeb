![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2022/04/15/theEggman.png)I generally am not a podcast reviewer, that is I don't review individual podcasts, except when I'm choosing one for [Blogger of the Year](https://daytona.scripting.com/search?q=%22Blogger%20of%20the%20Year%22), as I'm thinking of doing this year. But there's a whole class of podcasts that I am prepared to love that do it just plain wrong. Current example: The [official podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/pluribus-the-official-podcast/id1846198705) for [Pluribus](https://daytona.scripting.com/search?q=Pluribus). Previous example: The [official podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-severance-podcast-with-ben-stiller-adam-scott/id1788381175) for [Severance](https://daytona.scripting.com/search?q=Severance). The reason: It's a bunch of people laughing about how funny they are and how they are the best in the world at what they do. Or some seriously unfunny thing that happened or almost happened on the set. If a friend told you these stories you'd roll your eyes and ask them kindly then desperately to just move along please. They never criticize. Today I listened to the NYT [best-of 2025 in TV podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/sunday-special-the-best-tv-of-2025/id1200361736?i=1000742188278) by their critics, and it was imho exactly the way the official podcasts of hit shows should be. There has to be at least a possibility that they will say something critical, or funny irreverant even inconsiderate things, and not are not 100% self-promotion. The Pluribus podcast is just not interesing. Which is stupid because Pluribus is a very interesting series. I can't imagine too many people listen to the podcast, but then I can't imagine why lots of people do lots of things.

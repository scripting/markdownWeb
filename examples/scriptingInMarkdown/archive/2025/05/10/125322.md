We lived for the first years of my life in [Jackson Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_Heights), which is probably, today, the most culturally diverse place in the world. In the 1960s it was a white neighborhood that was adjacent to a black neighborhood, [Corona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona,_Queens). They paired our elementary schools when I was in third or fourth grade. My little brother went to the school in Corona, and I stayed in the school in Jackson Heights.

There's a [famous picture](http://scripting.com/images/2025/05/10/eveWinerPeterWinerWalkToSchool.png), in our family at least, of my mother walking my brother to school. It was on the front page of the NYT one day. That was a big deal then.

When I was in fifth grade we moved to [Flushing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing,_Queens). It was a sleepy little place (for NYC), nice houses, mostly Irish and Italian families with a few Jews. Now it's a Far East culture, from China and Korea, and still just as nice as it ever was.

We have a system in this country, it takes people from everywhere and gives them a platform to create whatever they want. In Flushing it's the best Chinese food in the city, which is saying a lot.

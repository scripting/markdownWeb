![](https://s3.amazonaws.com/scripting.com/images/2023/04/24/cheshireCat.png)Thanks to [Ben Werdmuller](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/technology/my-new-daily-blog/ar-AA1B2vJU?apiversion=v2&noservercache=1&domshim=1&renderwebcomponents=1&wcseo=1&batchservertelemetry=1&noservertelemetry=1) and [Om Malik](https://om.co/2025/03/16/my-new-daily-blog/) for their nice writeups of WordLand on their blogs. They're right. It is a small piece that can be hooked into lots of places, as is WordPress a big place that can host lots of apps many of which haven't been written yet. Products that look outward that can be hooked up in a million ways to everything, and leave the door open for those who follow. Such products are rare in our world. People always try to own their users by locking out competitors. I found a perfect spot for me to put some software, and I am having fun watching people use it, and coming up with new features that build on what we have. I think the writing tools market for WordPress will be huge, and I firmly believe that will turn into what I call [the writer's web](http://scripting.com/2025/03/13/132830.html), which you could also think of as just the web. Ben thinks of it as the "indie web," and that's fine. It's all just the web. Anyway, I should have put something here a long time ago, but I didn't look inside WordPress until a couple of years ago and I really liked what I found.

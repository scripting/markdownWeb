![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2020/11/23/heartNY.png)Yesterday's [Now & Then podcast](https://www.google.com/search?q=site%3Ascripting.com+%22Now+%26+Then%22+podcast&rlz=1C5CHFA_enUS743US747&sxsrf=AOaemvIVpAvY-txEFkhAmn7_YEpVusb3MQ%3A1632323867619&ei=G0lLYdWiJfKi_Qajqa-YAg&oq=site%3Ascripting.com+%22Now+%26+Then%22+podcast&gs_lcp=Cgdnd3Mtd2l6EAM6BwgAEEcQsANKBAhBGABQ7R1YzDJgjjZoAXACeACAAU6IAcMBkgEBM5gBAKABAcgBCMABAQ&sclient=gws-wiz&ved=0ahUKEwiVnKai8JLzAhVyUd8KHaPUCyMQ4dUDCA8&uact=5), about the history of NYC, was the first [episode](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/new-york-new-york/id1567665859?i=1000536112565) that was disappointing. At one point Heather Cox Richardson said, almost proudly, that the only thing she knows about NYC is the line in the song [New York, New York](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2u1zK8AaHic) "If you can make it there you can make it anywhere." This is one of my big problems with podcasts, the thing that tunes me out, when people speak from a position of false expertise. They're having a grand time, while I (the listener) am deciding whether or not to cut them off, based on the probability that they'll ever say anything interesting before the end. Yesterday I cut them off and listened to music for the second half of my ride. It's the same reason I can't watch any MSNBC show for more than 3 minutes. They're filling the air, meantime I know there are things going on they should be informing me of, and add to things my mind can think about. Okay so N&T had a bad episode, not the end of the world, the other most recent episodes are totally worth listening to, I highly recommend them, and I will keep listening in the hope that the NYC episode was just a bad idea they followed through on instead of canning.

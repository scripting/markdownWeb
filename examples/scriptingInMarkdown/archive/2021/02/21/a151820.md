![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2021/02/21/nomadland.png)I don't watch a lot of movies these days thanks to the pandemic I guess. But in the last two days I watched two, [NomadLand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomadland_\(film\)) and [I Care a Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Care_a_Lot). It's hard to imagine two more different movies. NomadLand is a character study, played by a 63-year-old [Frances McDormand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_McDormand). Her age is important. I understand this somewhat because I'm 65, and it's like that great [Al Pacino speech](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_Z_7eu64y6c) in [Any Given Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Any_Given_Sunday), as you get older [things are taken](https://youtu.be/_Z_7eu64y6c?t=102) from you. Parents, friends, lovers, and then pieces of yourself start going, and you're left wondering what the fuck. That's NomadLand in a nutshell. The acting is beautiful, the scenery, the characters, the feelings (somewhat) -- but the story? It's like viewing a work of art in a museum. It's just there. It's for you to decide what it means and a lot of that will depend on how old you are, I imagine. I Care a Lot is very much the opposite, but interestingly it's also about things being taken away from an old person, in this case played by 72-year-old [Dianne Wieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dianne_Wiest). So much happens in the first few minutes of the movie, and it's so anger-making, I turned it off at least three times to regain my composure to continue watching. But then it becomes a rollicking ultra-violent comedy, kind of like a Quentin Taratino movie, but funnier. If you want entertainment, I Care a Lot is for you. I will watch NomadLand again. Sometimes movies like this don't grab me on the first viewing, for example, [There Will Be Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_Will_Be_Blood), which i panned after a first viewing, then _loved_ after a second. I'll let you know.

![](https://imgs.scripting.com/2020/01/17/bagel.png)Airpods followup. As you may recall, I [lost one of my AirPod Pro's](http://scripting.com/2021/01/30.html#a162514) 10 days ago. I tried to order a replacement through the [Apple website](https://getsupport.apple.com/repair?caseid=101319750634&locale=en_US), but [the page](https://getsupport.apple.com/?caller=sfaq&PGF=PGF32003&category_id=SC0298&symptom_id=23360) appears to be broken. So I compromised, and bought a couple of less expensive bud-style headphones, one from [Jabra](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B082Y2KM92/ref=ppx_yo_dt_b_asin_title_o01_s01?ie=UTF8&psc=1), which comes highly recommended by [Wirecutter](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/reviews/best-wireless-earbuds/), and [Skullcandy](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B0857JLRFJ/ref=ppx_yo_dt_b_asin_title_o01_s00?ie=UTF8&psc=1). Together they cost about what the Airpod Pro's cost. But here's the thing, both of them sound awful on my iPhone. No bass at all, all high end. So I tried them on my Pixel 4a, same thing. My hearing is fine, I tried the one remaining AirPod Pro, and it sounds great. So now I'm trying to figure out how to equalize the audio on either of these phones. I can't believe there isn't a built-in equalizer, but it seems there's not. I really got to like the AirPods, didn't think I would. I find over-ear headphones clunky now.
